Independence and culture

Hairouna, the Land of the Blessed. Independent since 27 October 1979, with a culture built from Kalinago, Garifuna, African, European and Asian roots.

Independence Day, 27 October

On 27 October 1979, St. Vincent and the Grenadines became an independent nation after more than two centuries of British rule. Every October the country celebrates with parades and cultural events, and homes and streets fill with blue, gold and green.

National symbols

  • The flagBlue for the sky and sea, gold for sunshine and the warmth of the people, green for the islands' plant life. The three green diamonds in a V, for Vincent, are the "Gems of the Antilles". Adopted in 1985.
  • National birdThe St. Vincent parrot (Amazona guildingii), a big, brightly coloured parrot found nowhere else on Earth. Look for it on the Vermont Nature Trail.
  • National flowerThe Soufrière tree (Spachea perforata).
  • National dishRoasted breadfruit and fried jackfish. Breadfruit came to St. Vincent with Captain Bligh in 1793.
  • National HeroParamount Chief Joseph Chatoyer, Garifuna leader in the fight to keep the island, killed on 14 March 1795. National Heroes Day is 14 March.
  • Anthem and motto"St. Vincent, Land So Beautiful". The motto is Pax et Justitia: peace and justice.
Road to 50

Vincy Mas 2027

25 June to 6 July 2027

The national Carnival: soca and calypso, steel pan, J'ouvert at dawn and costumed bands taking over Kingstown. 2027 is the "Road to 50", marking 50 years since Carnival moved in 1977 from the days before Lent to June and July.

Culture you can join in

  • Nine MorningsThe nine mornings before Christmas: pre-dawn street parties, concerts and early sea baths, launched at Heritage Square in Kingstown. A uniquely Vincentian tradition.
  • Nine Nights of LightsThe Botanic Gardens open after dark for nine December nights of Christmas lights and music.
  • Garifuna heritageThe Garifuna people were exiled in 1797 to Baliceaux and then to Roatán, off Honduras. Their descendants live across Central America, and Heritage Month in March honours them at home.
  • Boat building in BequiaWooden boats, model boats and a sailing tradition you can see at the Easter Regatta and the Bequia Maritime Museum.
  • Easterval on Union IslandA celebration of Union's maroon culture, with the Culture Show and street parties every Easter week.
  • Friday limeJoin locals at Heritage Square in Kingstown for the TGIF lime.

A short history

  1. Before the 1700sThe Kalinago call the island Hairouna, the Land of the Blessed. The Garifuna people grow from Kalinago and African roots.
  2. 1763Britain takes control under the Treaty of Paris.
  3. 1765The Botanic Gardens are started, now among the oldest in the Western Hemisphere.
  4. 1793Captain Bligh brings breadfruit to St. Vincent.
  5. 1795Chatoyer is killed on 14 March during the Second Carib War.
  6. 1797The Garifuna are exiled to Baliceaux and then Roatán.
  7. 1834 to 1838Slavery is abolished, with full freedom in 1838.
  8. 1977Carnival moves to June and July, and Vincy Mas as we know it begins.
  9. 1979Independence on 27 October.
  10. 1985Today's flag, with its three green diamonds, is adopted.
  11. 2021La Soufrière erupts in April.
  12. 2027Vincy Mas celebrates its Road to 50.
